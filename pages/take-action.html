Take Action · By audience

Closing the gap takes more than planting — it takes keeping trees alive.

Pick the role closest to you. Each card has one thing you can do this month, one you can push for this year, and who to contact.

For Residents

If you live on a bare block

You don’t need acreage — a street tree in front of your building cools the sidewalk for a whole row.

  • This month: request a street tree

    Most U.S. cities plant on request via 311 / parks dept. Ask for a species suited to your sidewalk width. Example phrasing: “Request for street tree at [address], 4-ft planting strip, overhead wires present.”

  • This year: water the sapling

    New trees die without 15–20 gallons/week for two summers. A weekly bucket brigade with neighbors beats any one-time planting day.

  • Push for: cool routes to school

    Ask your council member to prioritize shade on the 500 m around schools and bus stops in low-canopy tracts.

For Local Government / Planners

If you control budget or right-of-way

Planting saplings is cheap; keeping mature canopy is the win. Protect, then fill, then maintain.

  • This month: audit by tract

    Join canopy LiDAR + ACS income + LST. Publish the gap map. Require that 60%+ of new plantings go to below-median canopy tracts.

  • This year: fund care, not just planting

    Write 3-year watering/pruning into the capital plan. Tie contractor pay to 2-year survival, not installation count.

  • Push for: code that keeps trees

    Tree protection ordinance + soil volume minimums (≥15 m³ per street tree) + light pavement on reconstructed blocks.

For Volunteers / Nonprofits

If you organize people

Stewardship is the bottleneck — groups that water and count keep canopy alive.

  • This month: run a stewardship census

    Map every street tree on two blocks (species, DBH, health). Share with the city — you just made the maintenance case measurable.

  • This year: adopt a cool corridor

    Pick one school-to-park route in a 7–14% tract. Plant consistently, mulch, water, and document temperature — then invite council to walk it in July.

  • Push for: paid youth crews

    Advocate for municipal youth forestry jobs — survival rates rise when care is compensated, not just volunteered.

How to use this site as a template for your city

  1. Replace js/data.js and assets/data/neighborhoods.json with your open-data extract (tract, canopy %, LST, income, AQI).
  2. Keep the equity ordering — bars remain sorted by income, maps tint by heat — so the thesis stays legible.
  3. Keep source notes on every chart, even when data is “sample.” Swap the note when you swap the data.

Equity reminder — carried on every page

Trees are not a perk to earn. Cool, breathable blocks are a baseline to share.

If your city’s budget already knows which blocks are hottest, it knows where to plant next. The question is whether it does.
