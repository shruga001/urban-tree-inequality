Urban Tree Inequality · Environmental Justice

Who gets shade, cleaner air & cooler streets?

In most U.S. metros, the leafiest blocks and the hottest blocks are only a few miles apart — and the gap follows income. Trees filter air, drop temperatures by 8–12°F, and cut energy bills. Where they are missing, heat illness and costs rise.

The pattern, in one street

Shade is infrastructure — and it is rationed.

Across the sample metro, the highest-income neighborhoods average 34–38% canopy and 31–32°C summer surfaces. The lowest-income average 7–9% canopy and 39–41°C. That 9°C gap is not random — it is zoning, redlining, and planting budgets compounding over decades.

Shade access

Street-tree spacing on affluent blocks: every 6–8 m. On bare blocks: every 40–60 m, if at all. School walk zones reflect the same gap.
